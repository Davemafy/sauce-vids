# Sauce: 200 potential-customer websites / research and test lab

Date: 2026-11-19. Dataset: https://devmcp.davemafy.xyz/sauce-motion-phase2/sauce-icp-200-20261119/

## What the list establishes

This is a public, sourced inventory of **200 plausible software-demo buyers or sites**, not 200 confirmed paying customers. No company has been contacted or confirmed willingness to buy Sauce. Sauce sells time saved creating professional, source-true software demonstration footage; it is not a general launch-film company or arbitrary website redesign service.

- 2,062 source entries were reviewed algorithmically from YC public startup batches Winter 2024–Fall 2026.
- Only active companies with listed software-related offerings and public website URLs entered the candidate pool. Industrial hardware, clinical services, robotics, agencies and nonsoftware companies were screened out. Keyword filtering and human exclusions remain imperfect; the lower tiers require further review.
- 27 indie SaaS launch candidates were added from SaaSearch's September and October 2026 reports, with 25 retained. Published plan labels are directory claims, not independently verified company revenue.
- **452 unique candidate domains** received a bounded public-homepage GET; **439 returned public HTML 2xx**; 10 responded with restricted/non-HTML content, 2 network errors and 1 TLS error.
- The selected final **200 distinct websites all returned public HTML 200** during this run. This only establishes homepage reachability, not an accessible working app or film permissions.
- Composition: **175 YC startups** and **25 indie SaaS launches**. Segments: developer tools 36; marketing/sales 34; AI workflows/agents 32; productivity/collaboration 28; finance/operations 22; design/creator 21; analytics/data 20; support/success 7.
- Research tiers: **A 30**, **B 70**, **C 100**. Ratings and demo-angle proposals are hypotheses, not empirical buying probabilities or observed app features.

## Concrete workflow to validate Sauce's business

1. **Buyer interview**: founder / devrel / small product-marketing team. Ask for the last real demo they published, hours spent retaking capture, launch frequency, edit cost, and purchase urgency. Avoid asking solely "do you like the idea?"
2. **Permissions**: choose a public or explicitly authorized product flow. A successful marketing homepage GET is NOT permission to record authenticated or third-party product data.
3. **Source-true feature**: validate one real action with a legible, commercially meaningful before/after. Do not select a workflow merely because it has a click event or large pixel delta.
4. **Creative first three seconds**: critique encoded frames at full 1080p and approximately 390px feed width against actual premium reference footage. Kill weak openings before full film renders.
5. **Paid pilot**: ask at least some qualified interested buyers to purchase an actual film/export. Track conversations, paid transactions and repeat use separately from technical success.

Stop-loss: after two failed visual studies on the same product action, change the source/workflow, not the shader. Green tests and HTTP 200 never count as a premium product film or a sale.
